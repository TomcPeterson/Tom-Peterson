FastPitch Softball Resume

Paul "Reggie" Burnett

My fastpitch softball career really began in my hometown of Vicksburg, Mississippi. At the age of nine, I made the Playground Summer League team at Alpine Heights playing with and against high school age players. I made the All Star team the next year at the age of 10. In the 14 year hiatus from fastpitch, I played baseball and was drafted by the New York Yankees at age 17 and had a successful career at Southern University.

My Iowa Fastpitch career started humbly at the Gates Park Monday Night League in 1980 and spread across North America until 1996. While watching the games at Gates Park, Roger Neebel and Hall of Fame Coach Jim Ciesielski approached me to fill in at shortstop for their team. After playing a few games in that League, the Denver Merchants approached me to play for them in the Northeast Iowa League. After hitting .320 for Denver in 1980, the team was sponsored by the Alibi Tavern from 1981 through 1983.

After hitting a home run in Westgate during a tournament, I stood and admired my work, reminiscent of Reggie Jackson, and Oscar Wooldrick called me "Reggie" from that day forward and the nickname has stuck. After hitting .350 during the 1981 and 1982 seasons where we played in the A State Tournament in Des Moines, I came back for my last season with the Alibi in 1983 and hit .375.

In 1985 I started a long playing career with Jim Ciesielski and his teams. Waterloo Turf Control sponsored the team in 1985 and in 1986 when while I hit third in the lineup, we qualified for the ASA Regionals in Omaha, NE.

Universal Financial Services sponsored the Waterloo team from 1987 to 1988. Theses Jim Ciesielski managed teams won back-to-back ISC Regional Championships at Walcott, Iowa and earned trips to the ISC World Tournament in Saskatoon, Saskatchewan, Canada in 1987 and Decatur, IL in 1988. In Decatur, we beat the former ASA National Champs Pay-N-Pak 2-1 in front of a standing room only crowd on "Diamond 2." I had the game winning RBI. It was the most satisfying victory of any kind that I have had in any sport in my career! Nobody gave us a chance. Universal Financial finished 15th in that Tournament.

I played in ASA State Tournaments from 1986 through 1992 and participated in ASA National Tournaments from 1989 through 1994. I was selected to the All-American team while playing for the Des Moines Metros in 1991 at the National Tournament in Midland, Michigan. I played in every ISC World Tournament from 1987 to 1992.

As my career was nearing an end, in 1996 I played in a Tournament in Middleton, Wisconsin as a favor to Steve Kaiser and I hit a home run in my last official at-bat. Other notable events in my career include, never missing a pitch that I swung at facing Peter Meredith. I’ve never hit .400 in my life - I’ve either hit in the .300s or in the .500s. In the magical season of 1992, I hit .568 in the Illinois Travel League and won the Batting Title while playing opposition such as the Bloomington Hearts, Taylorville, the Decatur Pride, and the Bloomington Beer Nuts. I finished the 1992 season with a batting average of .500 getting 105 hits in 210 at bats.

I always thought that if the pitching staff can "keep it close, we’ll find a way to win it!"
